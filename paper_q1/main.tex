\begin{abstract}
Speech emotion recognition (SER) systems are usually validated on uncompressed
audio and deployed on coded audio. Earlier studies examined the effect of this
gap on accuracy. This study examines its effect on the evidence a model uses.
Fifteen classifiers, including a small neural network, were fitted to 436
\emph{named} acoustic descriptors extracted from CREMA-D, with every clip
rendered as uncompressed WAV, MP3 at \SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second}, MP4/AAC at
\SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second}, and an AAC re-wrap that changes only the
container. The large cross-format losses are caused by train/serve mismatch
rather than by lost information: an RBF-SVM trained on WAV reaches 0.203
balanced accuracy on MP3, whereas the same model trained on MP3 reaches 0.589,
against 0.586 on clean audio. Under mismatch the neural network stops
predicting three of the six classes, a failure that balanced accuracy conceals.
Coding also changes the tests a model learns, though not the information those
tests extract. Entropy trees fitted per condition share the same root
descriptor, but the MP3 tree reproduces the reference structure only to depth 2
and the AAC tree only to depth 0, compared with depth 3 for the empty container
control. All four trees still extract the same total information gain to
within 2\,\%. Finally, a shift in feature importance is uninterpretable without
a null. The informationally empty re-wrap alone displaces 8.4 of a tree's 25
most important descriptors. Measured against that floor, the codec-specific
displacement is significant ($p\leq1.1\times10^{-3}$) but about 2.6 times
smaller than the uncalibrated figure. Attribution-shift claims in affective
computing should therefore be reported against a matched empty-perturbation
control.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Speech emotion recognition, explainable artificial intelligence, perceptual

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{udio} rarely reaches an inference endpoint in the format in
which it was captured. Contact-centre archives store MP3, streaming clients
negotiate Opus or AAC, and broadcast pipelines resample and requantise audio.
Benchmarks, in contrast, are distributed as uncompressed WAV and read directly
from disk. The difference is usually considered harmless because every
ingestion path decodes to linear PCM before features are extracted. Where this
assumption has been tested, it has been tested on accuracy, with mixed results:
Reddy and Vijayarajan~\cite{reddy2020compression} report codec effects on
speech emotion recognition (SER) that are real, modest and dependent on the
front end.

Accuracy alone is an incomplete measure for a system that must report both
\emph{what} it concluded and \emph{why}. A deployed affective system may present
an attribution over acoustic evidence to a clinician, an auditor or a
quality-assurance analyst, and that second output has robustness properties of
its own. Ghorbani et al.~\cite{ghorbani2019fragile} showed for image
classifiers that inputs which are indistinguishable to a human observer and
receive identical predictions can still receive very different attributions,
and Alvarez-Melis and Jaakkola~\cite{alvarez2018robustness} reported the same
fragility in a more general setting. Both studies used adversarial
perturbations. Transcoding is not adversarial, but it is routine, widespread
and seldom logged.

Studying the effect of coding on evidence requires a model whose evidence can be
inspected. An attribution placed on a latent dimension of an end-to-end audio
model does not correspond to a quantity an acoustician would recognise. A model
fitted to \emph{named} descriptors in the GeMAPS~\cite{eyben2016gemaps} and
openSMILE~\cite{eyben2010opensmile} tradition does: an attribution on
\ttf{prosody\_cpps} is a statement about breathiness, and a split on
\ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean} is a statement about the
\SIrange{6}{8}{\kilo\hertz} band. The study is restricted to this setting so
that every structural result is exact rather than estimated and can be checked
against an acoustic interpretation.

A claim that compression changed which features a model relies on is easy to
make and difficult to justify. Feature-importance rankings are unstable: two
descriptors that split the data almost equally well can exchange places, and a
single change near the root of a tree alters every node below it. Adebayo et
al.~\cite{adebayo2018sanity} and Krishna et al.~\cite{krishna2022disagreement}
have raised general concerns about attribution reliability, but measuring the
shift caused by a manipulation that carries no information is not yet standard
practice. The present design includes such a manipulation, namely the same
codec output routed through two different containers. Once that floor is
measured, most of the apparent codec effect on importance rankings is
attributable to generic sensitivity to perturbation.

The contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A matched-format decomposition of the codec effect. Training and serving
      in the same coded condition recovers a cross-format loss of up to 38.3
      balanced-accuracy points, which shows that \SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second}
      coding moves the affective information outside the fitted range without
      removing it. A per-class analysis shows that the mismatched network
      abandons half of the label space, which balanced accuracy cannot reveal
      (Sections~\ref{sec:mismatch}--\ref{sec:matched}).
\item Per-condition tree structure as an exact attribution. Entropy trees
      fitted independently per condition identify which test the codec
      changes, at the level of a named descriptor and a threshold. A depth
      profile quantifies how far that agreement extends, and evaluating
      information gain at every node separates structural from informational
      agreement (Sections~\ref{sec:trees}--\ref{sec:depth}).
\item Null calibration for shifts in feature importance. Three floors
      (tie-breaking, actor resampling, and an informationally empty container
      re-wrap) are measured, and a paired within-draw design separates the
      codec effect from all three (Section~\ref{sec:null}).
\item A repair that can be diagnosed and costs little. Neutralising a single
      named descriptor recovers 98\,\% of a kernel machine's loss and 92\,\% of
      a neural network's loss with no cost on clean audio
      (Section~\ref{sec:neutralise}).
\end{enumerate}
Two negative results are also reported: post-hoc attribution methods disagree
substantially on the same fitted model, and a global attribution ranking can
remain stable while the classifier stops working
(Sections~\ref{sec:posthoc} and~\ref{sec:stability}).

\section{Related Work}

\textit{Coding and speech task degradation.} Reddy and
Vijayarajan~\cite{reddy2020compression} found that standard codecs reduce SER
accuracy by an amount that depends on both the codec family and the feature
representation, which implies that coding interacts with the front end. In
neural codec benchmarking, Wu et al.~\cite{wu2024codecsuperb} showed that
signal-domain fidelity metrics do not predict whether task-relevant information
is retained: a codec can reconstruct a signal accurately while discarding the
paralinguistic content that downstream tasks require. Signal-domain
displacement and model behaviour are therefore reported here as separate
quantities.

\textit{Interpretability for tabular and audio models.} Post-hoc attribution
has converged on local surrogates~\cite{ribeiro2016lime}, Shapley-value
attribution~\cite{lundberg2017shap} with an exact algorithm for tree
ensembles~\cite{lundberg2020treeshap}, and permutation importance; for
differentiable models, path-integral~\cite{sundararajan2017ig} and
reference-based~\cite{shrikumar2017deeplift} methods are standard, with
consolidated implementations~\cite{kokhlikyan2020captum}. Surveys map the
field~\cite{guidotti2018survey}. Rudin~\cite{rudin2019stop} argues that
high-stakes decisions should use intrinsically interpretable models instead of
post-hoc explanations. The transfer of these methods to audio is debated.
Haunschmid et al.~\cite{haunschmid2020audiolime} argue that audio explanations
should be \emph{listenable}, and that applying image-segmentation logic to
spectrograms produces components with no perceptual correlate; related
formulations appear in~\cite{becker2018audiomnist,sotirou2024musiclime}. Nasr
et al.~\cite{nasr2025beyond} extend this critique to speech emotion.
Attribution over named descriptors avoids the objection by construction, at the
cost of restricting the model to hand-designed features.

\textit{Attribution stability.} Ghorbani et al.~\cite{ghorbani2019fragile}
showed that attributions can change substantially under imperceptible
perturbations while predictions remain fixed. Adebayo et
al.~\cite{adebayo2018sanity} showed that some saliency methods are independent
of both the model parameters and the data, and Krishna et
al.~\cite{krishna2022disagreement} documented frequent disagreement between
explanation methods applied to the same model. Taken together, these findings
indicate that attribution stability should be measured against a null rather
than assumed.

\textit{Distribution shift.} The mechanism identified in this study is
covariate shift~\cite{morenotorres2012shift}, with one unusual property: the
shifted variable is not a proxy for anything the label depends on, since
masking it has no cost on clean audio (Section~\ref{sec:neutralise}).

\textit{Research gap.} Coding effects have been characterised for accuracy but
not for evidence, and attribution fragility has been characterised for
adversarial perturbation of images but not for routine degradation of audio. No
prior work known to the author isolates the container under a verified
constraint of signal invariance, or calibrates a shift in feature importance
against a matched empty perturbation.

\section{Corpus, Stimuli, and Features}\label{sec:design}

\input{fig_framework}

\subsection{Corpus}
CREMA-D~\cite{cao2014cremad} comprises 7{,}442 clips from 91 actors, each
delivering one of twelve fixed sentences in one of six acted emotions (anger,
disgust, fear, happiness, neutral, sadness). Because the words are constant
across emotions, the lexical channel is removed, and any signal the models
recover must be carried by acoustic realisation, which is the property that
perceptual coding alters. The CREMA-D crowd raters, hearing audio alone,
matched the intended emotion on only 45.5\,\% of clips, with per-class recall
ranging from 0.966 (neutral) to 0.164 (sad). Models trained on the intended
label can exceed this figure because they exploit systematic acting cues; it is
reported as context rather than as a target.

\subsection{Reference construction and conditions}
The canonical reference is \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz} mono 16-bit PCM,
loudness-normalised to EBU~R128 ($I=\SI{-23}{\lufs}$, LRA${}=7$,
TP${}=\SI{-2}{\decibel}$). Each coded condition is produced from that reference
by a single FFmpeg encoder call (\ttf{libmp3lame -b:a 64k} for MP3;
\ttf{aac -b:a 64k -f mp4} for AAC), and every condition is returned to PCM
through one identical decoder call, so the decoder never confounds the codec.

The fourth condition allows the codec effect to be identified.
\ttf{roundtrip\_wav} is the AAC bitstream of \ttf{mp4\_aac64}, decoded and
re-wrapped as WAV, so it carries the same codec output in a different
container, with one additional int16 requantisation. Its median standardised feature difference from
\ttf{mp4\_aac64} is $7.6\times10^{-5}$, and only 12 of 436 descriptors exceed
0.05. Section~\ref{sec:null} uses it as an informationally empty perturbation.
Feature extraction over the $7{,}442\times4=29{,}768$ files failed on exactly
one clip in all four conditions (\ttf{1076\_MTI\_SAD\_XX}, documented as
containing no audio), leaving 7{,}441 clips.

\subsection{Feature representation}\label{sec:features}
Each file is reduced to 436 named descriptors in four families. Every
frame-level contour is summarised by eight order statistics (mean, standard
deviation, minimum, maximum, median, interquartile range, skewness, excess
kurtosis). The supplementary material defines and enumerates every column.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Spectral} (114): centroid, bandwidth, roll-off at 85 and 95\,\%,
flatness, flux, entropy, slope, zero-crossing rate, RMS energy, seven-band
spectral contrast, eight band-energy ratios, and high-frequency survival ratios
above 4 and \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz}.
\item \textbf{Cepstral} (240): 20 MFCCs with their $\Delta$ and $\Delta\Delta$.
\item \textbf{Prosodic} (56), from Praat via
Parselmouth~\cite{jadoul2018parselmouth}: $F_0$ statistics and slope, jitter
(local, RAP, PPQ5, DDP), shimmer (local, dB, APQ3, APQ5, APQ11, DDA),
harmonics-to-noise ratio, formants $F_1$--$F_5$ with bandwidths, intensity,
voiced-segment timing, and smoothed cepstral peak prominence (CPPS).
\item \textbf{Chroma} (24), plus clip duration and peak amplitude.
\end{itemize}
Spectral and cepstral descriptors are computed with
librosa~\cite{mcfee2015librosa} using a \SI{512}{\point} FFT, a
\SI{400}{\sample} window and a \SI{160}{\sample} hop (\SI{25}{\milli\second}
every \SI{10}{\milli\second}); MFCCs use 40 mel bands between 20 and
\SI{8000}{\hertz}. Praat uses a \SIrange{75}{500}{\hertz} pitch range and five
formants up to \SI{5500}{\hertz}. Chroma tuning is pinned to zero, because an
estimated tuning could itself shift under compression.

\textit{Partitioning.} Actors are split 60/11/20 into training, validation and
test sets with sex balance preserved (4{,}905, 896 and 1{,}640 clips), and the
same assignment and the same clips are used in every condition. Speaker
independence is necessary in this corpus: a variance decomposition gives a mean
$\eta^2$ of 0.178 for speaker against 0.098 for emotion, and 340 of the 436
descriptors are speaker-dominant.

\textit{Separability.} Of the 436 descriptors, 397 separate emotion at
Bonferroni-corrected significance. The strongest are \ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std}
($F=1194$), \ttf{mfcc\_00\_std} ($F=1159$) and \ttf{prosody\_intensity\_std}
($F=1078$). All three measure \emph{variability} rather than level, a pattern
that recurs in the attribution results. Fifty-one principal components are
required to explain 95\,\% of the variance, so the feature space is
high-dimensional.

\subsection{Effect of coding on the descriptors}\label{sec:signal}
The displacement caused by condition $c$ in a descriptor is measured by the
standardised mean difference against the training distribution,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smd}
|\mathrm{SMD}_c| = |\mu_c-\mu_{\text{ref}}|\,/\,\sigma_{\text{train}},
\end{equation}
which is read in the units that matter to a model fitted on the training
partition. Fig.~\ref{fig:signal} shows two distinct mechanisms.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_signal.pdf}
\caption{Codec displacement of the 436 descriptors. (a)~Most displaced descriptors per codec. (b)~CDF of $|\mathrm{SMD}|$. (c)~Duration added by each codec.}
\label{fig:signal}
\end{figure*}

MP3 removes most of the top octave. Perceptual bit allocation quantises the
highest band coarsely and sets masked bins to zero, so
\ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean} rises from 16.38 to 46.54 and mean spectral
flatness falls from 0.0189 to 0.0066. Expressed in training standard
deviations, the shift is $38.6\sigma$, which places the descriptor at inference
time in a range that no model fitted on clean audio has encountered.

AAC changes frame alignment instead. Encoder priming lengthens every clip by
\SI{32.68}{\milli\second}, which perturbs every frame-aligned temporal
derivative slightly without removing any single band. The largest AAC shifts
are therefore found in the delta-MFCC means, led by \ttf{mfcc\_d1\_01\_mean} at
$\mathrm{SMD}=-4.16$. Delta means lie near zero and have small standard
deviations, so their standardised shifts overstate the absolute change; the
duration measurement provides the stronger evidence for this mechanism.

\section{Models and Evaluation}\label{sec:models}

\subsection{Classifiers}
Fourteen classifiers were fitted, spanning tree
ensembles~\cite{breiman2001rf,chen2016xgboost}, kernel
machines~\cite{cortes1995svm}, linear and discriminant models, and
instance-based and probabilistic baselines~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn},
together with a PyTorch MLP (widths 512--256--128, batch normalisation, ReLU, dropout 0.3)
trained with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 128)
under a cosine schedule, class-weighted cross-entropy with label smoothing
0.05, and early stopping on validation balanced accuracy (patience 25, at most
200 epochs). Missing values are imputed with the training median and inputs are
standardised where the estimator requires it. For the cross-format experiments
(Sections~\ref{sec:leaderboard}--\ref{sec:mismatch}) the scikit-learn models
are tuned by randomised search over six candidates under three-fold
cross-validation grouped by speaker and scored by balanced accuracy. The
matched-format refits (Section~\ref{sec:matched}) use one fixed configuration
per model in every condition, with tree models grown on the entropy criterion.
Data augmentation is not used, because it would perturb the signal properties
under study. The global random seed is 42.

\subsection{Entropy and information gain}
The per-condition trees of Sections~\ref{sec:trees}--\ref{sec:null} are grown
with the Shannon-entropy criterion, so every reported split follows the rule
below exactly. For a node $S$ with class weights $w_c$ and $w=\sum_c w_c$, the node
entropy in bits is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
H(S) \;=\; -\sum_{c=1}^{6} p_c \log_2 p_c,
\qquad p_c \;=\; \frac{w_c}{w}.
\end{equation}
A test on descriptor $f$ at threshold $\tau$ partitions $S$ into
$S_L=\{x: x_f\leq\tau\}$ and $S_R$ and is scored by the information gain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ig}
\mathrm{IG}(S,f,\tau) \;=\; H(S) \;-\; \frac{w_L}{w}H(S_L) \;-\; \frac{w_R}{w}H(S_R),
\end{equation}
and the tree selects the $(f,\tau)$ that maximises it. Class weights are
\texttt{balanced}, so the root entropy of every tree is
$\log_2 6=2.5850$ bits. On uncompressed audio the best root test is
$\ttf{prosody\_intensity\_std}\leq8.2184$, which sends 3{,}098.67 of the
4{,}905 weighted samples left with $H(S_L)=2.3849$ and $H(S_R)=2.0891$ bits,
for $\mathrm{IG}=0.3090$ bits. The most informative single test on a CREMA-D
clip therefore concerns the variability of its loudness, and this test resolves
12\,\% of the label uncertainty.

\subsection{Agreement measures and the excess shift}\label{sec:measures}
An attribution $E$ is a vector over the 436 descriptors. Two attributions are
compared by \emph{top-25 retention}, the number of descriptors shared by their
25 highest-ranked entries, and by Spearman $\rho$ computed over the descriptors
that at least one of the two fits actually uses (over all columns, $\rho$ is
inflated by the roughly 130 that both trees leave at zero). Two trees are
compared by aligning internal nodes on their \emph{path} from the root, a string
of \ttf{L} and \ttf{R} branches, since the path is the only node identifier
with the same meaning in two trees fitted on different data. \emph{Descriptor
agreement} at a depth is the fraction of path-aligned nodes selecting the same
descriptor, and the \emph{deepest identical level} is the greatest depth to
which two trees agree at every node (0 means only the root is shared; $-1$
means even the root differs).

Let $A(E,E')$ be an agreement statistic, $E_{\text{ref}}$ and $E_c$ the
attributions of models fitted on the reference and on condition $c$, and $A_0$
the agreement obtained under a manipulation that carries no coding
information. The codec effect is reported as the \emph{excess} shift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excess}
\Delta_c \;=\; A_0 \;-\; A(E_{\text{ref}},E_c),
\end{equation}
rather than as the raw drop from a clean refit, because the raw drop attributes
to the codec any movement that the estimator would have produced regardless.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
Whenever a floor and an effect are compared, both are measured within the same
resampled training set. On each of 20 draws, 51 of the 60 training actors are
sampled without replacement; the reference tree is fitted twice under different
tie-breaking seeds ($2000+d$ and $9000+d$ on draw $d$), and each coded tree once
on the same actors. Paired per-draw differences are tested with the Wilcoxon
signed-rank test and reported with the paired effect size $d_z$. The accuracy
tables are derived from a single speaker-independent split and are not tested
statistically. Differences below about three points are not interpreted, and
every difference on which a conclusion depends lies between 10 and 38 points.

Named descriptors make the mechanism readable. A tree tests whether
\ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean} exceeds a threshold near 17, and the outcome
is the same whether the value is 16.4 or 46.5, whereas a linear or kernel model
propagates the $38\sigma$ excursion directly into its decision function. The
two groups are named by this property rather than by model family, because
$k$NN and Gaussian NB apply no learned weight; in all eight magnitude-sensitive
models the \emph{magnitude} of the descriptor enters the decision continuously,
while the seven tree-based models only test on which side of a threshold it
falls. AAC behaves differently. It reduces the accuracy of every model, but
only moderately (0.7 to 11 points regardless of family), which is consistent
with the diffuse padding mechanism of Fig.~\ref{fig:signal}(c). Container
routing has no material effect: across the fifteen models the roundtrip and
\ttf{mp4\_aac64} scores differ by a median of 0.003 and at most 0.010.

\subsection{Per-condition tree splits}\label{sec:trees}
Equal accuracy does not imply equal evidence. Fig.~\ref{fig:trees} shows the
top three levels of an entropy tree fitted independently on each condition,
with the node entropy and information gain given at every node. The
supplementary material lists every internal node to depth 4.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_trees.pdf}
\caption{Top three levels of the entropy tree fitted on each condition, with node entropy $H$ and information gain in bits.}
\label{fig:trees}
\end{figure*}

The primary evidence is unchanged. All four trees select
\ttf{prosody\_intensity\_std} at the root, with an information gain of 0.3090
bits under WAV and MP3 and 0.3064 bits under AAC.

The root threshold moves in a way that matches the coding mechanism. MP3 shifts
it by 0.03\,\% (8.2184 to 8.2210) and AAC by 1.29\,\% (to 8.3241). The
\SI{32.68}{\milli\second} of near-silent priming increases the dispersion of the
intensity contour, and the tree compensates with a higher threshold, so the
padding mechanism of Fig.~\ref{fig:signal}(c) is visible as a parameter of the
decision rule.

The trees diverge at depth 1 under AAC but not under MP3. The WAV and MP3 trees
both split the quieter branch on \ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std} (thresholds 3.9965 and
4.0175). The AAC and roundtrip trees instead select \ttf{mfcc\_00\_iqr}, a
\emph{static} dispersion measure of the same coefficient, with almost identical
gain (0.1408 against 0.1415 bits). A frame-alignment perturbation corrupts the
temporal derivatives but leaves the distribution of the contour intact, and the
tree selects an equally informative test that the codec has not affected.

The container control is reproduced at the level of tree structure.
\ttf{mp4\_aac64} and \ttf{roundtrip\_wav} select identical descriptors at all
seven nodes, with a root threshold of 8.3241 in both and depth-1 thresholds of
60.5853 and 60.5802. The distinction between container and codec, previously
observable only as a difference in accuracy, is thus observable in the learned
decision rule itself.

\subsection{Depth of structural agreement}\label{sec:depth}
Agreement at the root concerns a single node out of many. The same tree was
therefore grown to depth 6 in each condition (55 to 61 internal nodes, with a
25-sample leaf minimum), and path-aligned nodes were compared
(Section~\ref{sec:measures}). Because tree growth is greedy, the top three
levels are identical, node for node, to the trees of Fig.~\ref{fig:trees}. As
Fig.~\ref{fig:depth} shows, agreement decays with depth in every comparison, at
a rate that depends on the codec.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_depth.pdf}
\caption{Tree agreement with depth. (a)~Share of path-aligned nodes selecting the same descriptor. (b)~Deepest identical level over 20 paired draws.}
\label{fig:depth}
\end{figure*}

The estimator itself is not the limiting factor. Refitting on identical audio
with a different tie-breaking seed leaves every node to depth 4 unchanged and
displaces one node of 30 at depth 5, averaged over a panel of ten seeds.

An informationally empty perturbation costs three levels of agreement. The
container re-wrap reproduces the reference tree exactly to depth 3, and then
agrees on 11 of 16 nodes at depth 4 and on 12 of 23 at depth 5. Below depth 3,
the structure of this tree is therefore not a property of the audio, and the
calibration of Section~\ref{sec:null} applies here as well.

Both codecs fall below that floor, in the order predicted by their mechanisms.
MP3 reproduces the reference to depth 2 and AAC only to depth 0.
Under paired resampling the tie-breaking floor reaches a mean deepest identical
level of $5.10\pm1.21$ and the container floor $2.30\pm0.57$
($p=1.2\times10^{-4}$, $d_z=2.06$). MP3 reaches $0.90\pm0.91$, below the
container floor ($p=3.5\times10^{-4}$, $d_z=1.34$), and AAC $-0.05\pm0.51$
($p=8.7\times10^{-5}$, $d_z=2.89$). AAC displaces the structure more than MP3
($p=1.1\times10^{-3}$, $d_z=1.15$), which reproduces the ranking result of
Section~\ref{sec:null} on a different statistic. \ttf{mp4\_aac64} and
\ttf{roundtrip\_wav} yield the same divergence depth in all 20 draws.

The information extracted by the trees is preserved even where their structure
is not. Table~\ref{tab:entropy} evaluates Eqs.~\eqref{eq:entropy} and~\eqref{eq:ig} at
every internal node of the four depth-6 trees. Mean node entropy follows almost
the same trajectory in every condition (no per-depth mean differs from WAV by
more than 0.064 bits). Summed over six levels the WAV tree extracts 0.8754 of
the 2.5850 available bits, MP3 0.8714 ($-0.46\,\%$) and AAC 0.8590
($-1.87\,\%$). The container re-wrap extracts 0.8578 bits, the lowest of the
four despite carrying no coding information, so differences of this size lie
within the floor. Below depth 2 the AAC tree shares almost no structure with the
reference, yet it resolves the same label uncertainty to within 2\,\%. The codec
therefore changes which tests the tree selects without reducing the information
those tests extract. The depth-1 substitution described in
Section~\ref{sec:trees} occurs throughout the tree.

feature space, codec invariance is therefore a property of the first two or

the model as a whole.

\subsection{Null calibration of the importance ranking}\label{sec:null}
Taken at face value, the importance rankings suggest a large effect: the top
25 descriptors of the tree under MP3 share only 12 members with those under WAV,
with $\rho=0.017$. This interpretation is not supported, because the deep
importance ranking of a tree is fragile. Three floors were measured on the same
estimator: tie-breaking (data fixed), actor bootstrap (codec fixed at WAV) and
the container re-wrap (codec output fixed). In a marginal comparison,
resampling the actors on which the tree is fitted retains only 8.0 of the 25
descriptors ($\rho=-0.200$), fewer than any codec condition, so every codec
effect falls within the sampling floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:nullcal}(a)). That
comparison, however, varies two factors at once. Floor and effect were
therefore measured within the same draw (Section~\ref{sec:models}), with the
results given in Table~\ref{tab:paired} and Fig.~\ref{fig:nullcal}(b).

description of the evidence the model requires.

The codec effect is significant but amounts to about two fifths of its apparent

band around which the tree can set a threshold.

\subsection{Post-hoc attribution and its disagreements}\label{sec:posthoc}
Attribution was computed on the held-out speakers with TreeSHAP for the
ensembles~\cite{lundberg2020treeshap}, LinearSHAP for linear models,
LIME~\cite{ribeiro2016lime}, permutation importance, and a depth-4 global
surrogate tree. Six Captum methods~\cite{kokhlikyan2020captum} were applied to
the network: Saliency, Input$\times$Gradient,
Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017ig},
DeepLIFT~\cite{shrikumar2017deeplift}, GradientSHAP and Feature Ablation.

Different model families converge on similar evidence. On uncompressed audio,
the top five SHAP
descriptors of XGBoost and LightGBM are identical (\ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std},
\ttf{mfcc\_d2\_00\_std}, \ttf{duration\_s}, \ttf{prosody\_intensity\_std},
\ttf{mfcc\_00\_std}), and all six gradient methods return
\ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std}, \ttf{mfcc\_d2\_00\_std}, \ttf{prosody\_cpps} and
\ttf{duration\_s}. The two leading descriptors measure the variability of the
rate of change of overall loudness, which corresponds closely to vocal
agitation. Attribution mass follows column share: MFCCs make up 55\,\% of the
columns and receive 44 to 57\,\% of the attribution across models, so they have
no advantage per descriptor. The decision tree relies most heavily on prosody
(31\,\% against a column share of 13\,\%), which makes it readable but also
limits its accuracy. The per-emotion attributions in
Table~\ref{tab:peremotion} agree with the speech literature: CPPS, the standard
correlate of breathy phonation, ranks first for sadness, median $F_0$ for fear,
and $F_2$ movement for disgust. Since the network received no phonetic
supervision, this agreement serves as a construct-validity check on the
attribution. It also indicates which emotions the codecs are likely to affect:
\ttf{prosody\_cpps} leaves the top 25 descriptors of the network under MP3
(Section~\ref{sec:stability}), and \ttf{duration\_s}, which ranks first for
disgust and happiness, is the descriptor that AAC priming shifts on every
clip.

their correctness. Only descriptors identified by all four independent views
should be reported as robust; in this study they are \ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std},

rank-deficient and diverged (maximum mean $|$SHAP$|$ of $2.1\times10^{12}$
against a median of $5.3\times10^{-4}$, with whole descriptor families at exactly
zero). This estimate is excluded, and the SVM contributes accuracy results but
no SHAP attribution.

\subsection{Attribution stability under coding}\label{sec:stability}
Table~\ref{tab:stability} holds each fitted model fixed and changes only its
input. This differs from Section~\ref{sec:null}, in which the model is
refitted: the split structure of a fitted tree does not change when its inputs
change slightly, whereas refitting can arrive at an equally good structure by a
different route. Tree and linear attributions change very little, while those
of the network change substantially. Accuracy nevertheless understates the
change in attribution: XGBoost loses
2.2 points on AAC and swaps 3 of its top 25 descriptors, and the network loses
8.7 points and swaps 9. Under MP3, the descriptors that \emph{enter} the top 25
of the network are almost all encoder artefacts (\ttf{spectral\_flatness} in
five statistics, and \ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean}), while those that
\emph{leave} are voice-quality and prosodic measures (\ttf{prosody\_cpps},
\ttf{prosody\_f0\_skew}, \ttf{prosody\_f2\_mean}, \ttf{prosody\_f2\_std},
\ttf{prosody\_f3\_bw\_median}). Under mismatch, the network therefore shifts its
reliance from properties of the voice to artefacts of the encoder.

\subsection{Diagnosis and repair}\label{sec:neutralise}
Because the descriptors are named, the failure can be corrected. Descriptors
were ranked by $|\mathrm{SMD}|$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:smd}), and the $k$ most shifted
were replaced with their training medians at inference only, in both the coded and
the clean test sets (Table~\ref{tab:neutralise}). The single descriptor masked
at $k=1$ is \ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean}. Masking it alone recovers 98\,\%
of the RBF-SVM's 38.3-point loss and 92\,\% of the network's 24.6-point loss;
masking three brings logistic regression to 0.562 against a clean 0.567.

identifiable descriptors rather than a loss of emotional information, which is
the conclusion reached independently in Section~\ref{sec:matched}.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Separating the effects of coding}
The results separate four questions that a single accuracy figure combines.
First, the codec does not destroy the affective information, since matched
training comes within 0.003 of the clean score. Second, the codec does impair
deployed models, severely so when they use descriptor magnitudes and were
fitted on clean audio. Third, the codec changes what the model learns, although
the leading tests are preserved: the root test is invariant, MP3 leaves two
further levels intact, and the structure below is reorganised. Fourth, this
reorganisation does not cost information. Below depth 2 the AAC tree shares
almost no structure with the reference, yet it resolves the same label
uncertainty (0.859 against 0.875 bits). The codec substitutes one test for
another of equal value. This is a more specific finding than a general change
in attributions, and it can be established only because the splitting
criterion is explicit and the descriptors are named. Cross-format benchmarking,
which addresses only the second question, would conclude that MP3 at
\SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second} is unsuitable for SER, when the actual problem
lies in the pipeline. The per-class analysis shows the opposite risk, in which
an aggregate score conceals a consequential change: a balanced accuracy of 0.354
describes a model that has become, without any visible signal, a three-way
classifier.

\subsection{The need for null calibration}
The container re-wrap carries no coding information, yet it changes a third of
the top-25 ranking of the tree and removes its node-for-node identity below
depth 3. A claim that a manipulation changed which features a model uses cannot
be interpreted without a perturbation matched in every respect except
informational content. Such a control is proposed here as standard practice
for feature-attribution claims in affective computing. It requires one
additional condition and one additional fit, and it makes the claim
falsifiable. The same control absorbed a large part of the apparent codec
effect on two statistics computed from different objects (an importance ranking
and a split structure), and both statistics agree on the ordering of the two
codecs, which is the strongest evidence in this study that the codec-specific
remainder is real. The paired design is as important as the null itself: in a
marginal comparison, the actor-resampling floor exceeds every codec effect, and
no effect could have been reported.

\subsection{Implications for deployment and auditing}
Under mismatch, axis-aligned models are almost fully robust to a $38\sigma$
excursion, which is a real and inexpensive engineering advantage. That
advantage disappears under matched training, however, and the learned
structure of the same models is \emph{more} sensitive to a small diffuse
perturbation than to a large concentrated one. With matched training, the
accuracy of the depth-6 tree differs by only 1.6 points between MP3 and AAC,
whereas the depth to which each reproduces the reference structure differs by
two levels. Threshold-based models therefore protect the decision but not the
evidence behind it.

This distinction matters wherever per-instance rationales are presented to
operators. If an audit is carried out on archived compressed material while
inference runs on uncompressed input, or the reverse, the decisions will agree
but the explanations will not. An explanation is reproducible only if the
delivery format is recorded, and no SER benchmark known to the author reports
it. A rationale based on the root test is reproducible in every condition
studied here, whereas a rationale based on a rule several levels down, or on a
ranked list of secondary features, is not. Rationales intended to withstand
scrutiny should be limited to the depth to which the structure has been shown
to reproduce for the format in use.

The repair described in Section~\ref{sec:neutralise} has no counterpart in a
system with a learned front end, because identifying and neutralising the
affected channel required units with acoustic meaning. This position is
narrower than that of Rudin~\cite{rudin2019stop}. The results do not suggest
that interpretable models should replace stronger ones, since the best model in
this study is a neural network. They suggest instead that a front end based on
named descriptors retains a capacity for diagnosis and repair that end-to-end
pipelines lack. In this study, that capacity recovered 98\,\% of a 38-point loss
by masking a single column.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
CREMA-D contains acted emotion on 12 fixed sentences, so the absolute
accuracies do not transfer to spontaneous speech, and the human voice-only rate
of 45.5\,\% shows that the labels are only partly recoverable from audio. The
accuracy tables are based on a single speaker-independent split rather than
repeated cross-validation; only the null-calibration and depth analyses use
resampling. Only MP3 and AAC at \SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second} were tested. The
MP3 result is severe partly because this bitrate is aggressive for that codec,
and Opus was not evaluated. Summary statistics discard temporal information, so
no attribution reported here can localise \emph{when} in a clip the evidence
occurs. The per-condition and null analyses were designed after the
cross-format results had been inspected, so the study is exploratory rather
than confirmatory.

The measurement instruments have further limitations. The container control
differs from \ttf{mp4\_aac64} by an int16 requantisation, so it is an almost
empty rather than an exactly empty perturbation. This error is conservative,
because an exact null would lower the floor and enlarge the codec effects. The
null calibration and the depth profile cover a single readable tree, and it
remains open whether ensemble or gradient attributions require floors of the
same size, although Table~\ref{tab:stability} suggests that they are more
stable. The attribution comparisons are global, and
Section~\ref{sec:stability} shows that such comparisons can miss a complete
loss of accuracy. Finally, because of speaker dominance ($\eta^2$ of 0.178
against 0.098 for emotion), part of any importance ranking reflects which
voices are present in the training set.

Several extensions follow from these limitations. A bit-exact container null,
formed by two lossless containers carrying identical PCM, would tighten the
floor. Extending null calibration to refitted SHAP and integrated-gradients
rankings would show whether the observed fragility is specific to trees. A
bitrate ladder that includes Opus would turn the two-codec comparison into a
dose-response curve, for which the deepest identical level provides a
convenient single-number summary. A per-instance stability analysis would
address the limitation identified in Section~\ref{sec:stability}. Beyond
emotion, speaker identification and voice-quality assessment depend on the
spectral detail that perceptual coding discards, and the matched-format result
may not transfer to those tasks.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper examined whether perceptual audio coding changes the evidence on
which a speech emotion model relies, using models whose evidence consists of
named acoustic descriptors. Coding does not remove the affective information: a
kernel machine that reaches 0.203 on MP3 when fitted to clean audio reaches
0.589 when fitted to MP3, against 0.586 on clean audio. Cross-format losses are
caused by train/serve mismatch and can be corrected either by matched training
or by neutralising a single affected descriptor. Coding does change the
structure a model learns. The root test and its information gain are invariant,
but MP3 reproduces the reference structure only to depth 2 and AAC only to
depth 0, while the total information extracted remains within 2\,\%.

The size of this change is easily overstated. An informationally empty
container re-wrap alone displaces 8.35 of the 25 most important descriptors of a
tree, and only the difference beyond that floor, 3.25 descriptors for MP3 and
5.15 for AAC, can be attributed to the codec. Claims that an intervention
changed a model's explanation should therefore be accompanied by the divergence
produced by an intervention that carries no information. Without that
comparison such claims cannot be evaluated; with it, the codec effect reported
here is reduced by a factor of about 2.6.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This study uses a published corpus of consenting professional actors performing
scripted sentences, collected and released for research by its original
authors~\cite{cao2014cremad}; no new recordings were made. Speech emotion
recognition is a dual-use technology. The findings concern robustness and the
validity of explanations, and they caution against deploying such systems on
heterogeneous audio without format-aware validation. Nothing in this paper
should be taken as evidence that automatic emotion inference is accurate, fair
or appropriate in consequential settings.
