\begin{abstract}
Speech emotion recognition is validated on uncompressed audio and deployed on
coded audio. The gap is assumed benign because every ingestion path decodes to
PCM before inference. We test that assumption on the quantity accuracy cannot
see: the evidence a model actually uses. Two arms share one canonical reference
signal derived from CREMA-D and one coding manipulation (MP3 at
\SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second}). Arm~A probes a commercial multimodal audio
language model through occlusion attribution over spectro-temporal regions;
Arm~B trains fifteen interpretable classifiers and networks on 436
named acoustic features and reads their evidence with SHAP, LIME, permutation
importance, partial dependence, global surrogates, and six gradient-based
attribution methods. Both arms are calibrated against a null: a
\SI{1}{\lsb}-dither perturbation in Arm~A, a decode-path control in Arm~B.
Four results survive that calibration. First, coding relocates evidence more
than it relocates accuracy: the language model shows no detectable loss of
discriminability (AUC 0.734 to 0.746, all intervals overlapping) while its
attribution rank concordance falls to $\rho=0.491$ against a measured null floor
of $0.699$ ($p=4.2\times10^{-8}$); in the feature arm, XGBoost loses
2.2~accuracy points on AAC yet replaces three of its twenty-five
most-attributed features. Second, explanation stability is a property of the
model family, not of the codec, and it is ordered by how a model consumes its
inputs --- tree ensembles $\rho\geq0.98$, linear models $0.94$--$0.99$, the
neural network $0.677$, the language model $0.491$. Third, both arms
independently localise the damage to \SIrange{4}{8}{\kilo\hertz}, and the same
physical distortion produces opposite behavioural outcomes depending on whether
a model thresholds that band or multiplies it. Fourth, the container is inert:
substituting an MP3 for its bit-identical decoded WAV moves the language model's
posterior less than one least significant bit of noise
($0.83\times$ the null floor), and re-wrapping AAC as WAV moves the fifteen
feature models by a median of 0.003~balanced accuracy. Because Arm~B's evidence is named,
the mechanism is diagnosable and repairable: masking a single feature,
\ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean}, recovers 98\,\% of an RBF-SVM's
38.3-point loss at no cost on clean audio. We argue that attribution stability
is a reportable robustness property distinct from accuracy, that it requires a
null perturbation to be interpretable at all, and that named-feature pipelines
retain a diagnostic handle that end-to-end systems do not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{udio} almost never reaches an inference endpoint in the
representation in which it was captured. Contact-centre archives hold MP3;
streaming clients negotiate Opus or AAC; broadcast pipelines resample and
requantise; a clip may be transcoded three times between microphone and model.
Evaluation practice runs the other way: benchmarks are distributed as
uncompressed WAV and read directly from disk.

The gap is usually dismissed on a reasonable-sounding argument. Any ingestion
path decodes to linear PCM before feature extraction, so the delivery format is
an implementation detail with no behavioural consequence. Where the argument has
been tested at all, it has been tested on accuracy, and accuracy is often
reassuring: Reddy and Vijayarajan~\cite{reddy2020compression} report that codec
effects on speech emotion recognition (SER) are real but modest and
front-end-dependent.

Accuracy is the wrong instrument for the question we care about. A deployed
affective system is increasingly required to say not only \emph{what} it
concluded but \emph{why} --- an attribution over acoustic evidence, surfaced to
a clinician, an auditor, or a quality-assurance analyst. That second output has
its own robustness properties, and they are not implied by the first.
Ghorbani et al.~\cite{ghorbani2019fragile} established for image classifiers
that perceptually indistinguishable inputs receiving identical predictions can
be assigned materially different attributions; Alvarez-Melis and
Jaakkola~\cite{alvarez2018robustness} showed the same fragility more generally.
Both used adversarially constructed perturbations. A transcode is not
adversarial. It is routine, ubiquitous, and nobody logs it.

\subsection{Two estimands}
The question therefore decomposes into two that admit different answers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does perceptual coding degrade task performance?
\item Does coding alter the \emph{evidential basis} of the model's output,
      independently of whether the output changes?
\end{enumerate}
Answering the second requires a model whose evidence can be inspected, and
inspectability is not a single property. It is a spectrum. At one end sits a
commercial audio language model reachable only through a chat-completions
endpoint, where attribution must be estimated by perturbing the input. At the
other sits a decision tree over named acoustic descriptors, where the evidence
is the model. Prior work on codec robustness has occupied one end or the other.
Neither end alone can separate what the codec does to the signal from what the
model does with the codec.

\subsection{Contributions}
This paper places both ends of that spectrum on a single controlled
manipulation and reports what each can and cannot see.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A two-arm design on one canonical reference.} Both arms derive
      every stimulus from the same EBU~R128-normalised \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz}
      monophonic reference and share the MP3-at-\SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second}
      contrast, so cross-arm statements are about model class rather than about
      differing signal chains (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
\item \textbf{Null calibration on both sides.} Arm~A calibrates its perturbation
      estimator against an inaudible \SI{1}{\lsb} dither; Arm~B calibrates
      against a decode-path control in which container routing changes but the
      codec output does not. Every effect is reported relative to its own floor
      (Sections~\ref{sec:armA}, \ref{sec:armB}).
\item \textbf{Evidence that explanation displacement exceeds accuracy
      displacement, and how far the claim extends.} The strong dissociation ---
      unchanged accuracy, changed evidence --- is observed in Arm~A but does not
      reproduce in Arm~B. The weaker and still consequential form holds in both:
      the accuracy delta systematically understates the attribution delta
      (Section~\ref{sec:synthesis}).
\item \textbf{A mechanism that only the two arms together supply.} Both
      independently localise coding damage to \SIrange{4}{8}{\kilo\hertz}.
      In Arm~A that band carries modest reliance, so sensitivity survives; in
      Arm~B the same band enters models that multiply it, and they collapse.
      One physical cause, two opposite behavioural outcomes, mediated by model
      family (Section~\ref{sec:synthesis}).
\item \textbf{A diagnosable and cheap repair, available only where features are
      named.} Neutralising a single top-band descriptor recovers 98\,\% of an
      RBF-SVM's loss and 92\,\% of a neural network's, at no cost on
      uncompressed audio (Section~\ref{sec:neutralise}).
\item \textbf{Two negative results retained rather than smoothed.} Post-hoc
      attribution methods disagree substantially with each other on the same
      fitted model, and a global attribution ranking can report ``nothing
      changed'' while the classifier has stopped working
      (Sections~\ref{sec:methods-disagree}, \ref{sec:blindspot}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Perceptual coding and speech task degradation}
Degradation of speech systems under lossy coding is documented but shallowly
characterised. Reddy and Vijayarajan~\cite{reddy2020compression} find that
standard codecs reduce SER accuracy with a magnitude conditional on both codec
family and feature representation, which already implies that coding interacts
with the front end rather than degrading performance uniformly.

Of greater methodological consequence is a result from neural codec
benchmarking. Wu et al.~\cite{wu2024codecsuperb} demonstrate that signal-domain
fidelity metrics \emph{fail to predict} the retention of task-relevant
information: a codec can score well under reconstruction error while discarding
paralinguistic content that downstream tasks require. We take this seriously
enough to report signal fidelity and model behaviour as separate quantities
throughout, treating neither as a surrogate for the other.

\subsection{Interpretability for tabular and audio models}
Post-hoc attribution has converged on a small set of workhorses: local surrogate
fitting~\cite{ribeiro2016lime}, Shapley-value
attribution~\cite{lundberg2017shap} with an exact polynomial-time algorithm for
tree ensembles~\cite{lundberg2020treeshap}, and occlusion
sensitivity~\cite{zeiler2014visualizing} for models exposed only through their
input--output interface. For differentiable models, path-integral
methods~\cite{sundararajan2017ig} and reference-based
decompositions~\cite{shrikumar2017deeplift} are standard, and are available in
consolidated form~\cite{kokhlikyan2020captum}. Broad surveys map the
space~\cite{guidotti2018survey}; Rudin~\cite{rudin2019stop} argues that for
high-stakes decisions the post-hoc layer should be replaced by an intrinsically
interpretable model, a position our Section~\ref{sec:leaderboard} result speaks
to directly and unfavourably.

Transfer of these methods to audio is contested. Haunschmid et
al.~\cite{haunschmid2020audiolime} argue that audio explanations should satisfy
a \emph{listenability} criterion and that transplanting image-segmentation logic
onto time--frequency representations yields components with no perceptual
correlate; their remedy substitutes source-separated stems as the interpretable
basis. Related formulations appear in~\cite{becker2018audiomnist}
and~\cite{sotirou2024musiclime}, and Nasr et al.~\cite{nasr2025beyond} extend
the critique to speech emotion specifically, observing that a salient
time--frequency region is not thereby an acoustically meaningful one. Source
separation is inapplicable to monophonic single-speaker material, so Arm~A
adopts temporal windows and frequency bands --- the representation under
criticism --- and mitigates rather than dismisses (Section~\ref{sec:armA-attr}).
Arm~B sidesteps the objection entirely by attributing over named acoustic
descriptors in the tradition of GeMAPS~\cite{eyben2016gemaps}
and openSMILE~\cite{eyben2010opensmile}, where every unit of attribution has a
phonetic reading by construction.

\subsection{Attribution stability as a validity requirement}
The literature most directly load-bearing for this paper concerns the fragility
of attribution itself. Ghorbani et al.~\cite{ghorbani2019fragile} show that
attributions can be substantially altered by perturbations below the threshold
of perception while predictions and confidences remain stable. Adebayo et
al.~\cite{adebayo2018sanity} demonstrate that certain widely deployed saliency
methods are statistically independent of both model parameters and
data-generating process, producing outputs with the surface form of explanation
and none of its content. Krishna et al.~\cite{krishna2022disagreement} document
that different explanation methods routinely disagree on the same model, and
that practitioners resolve the disagreement by unprincipled means.

The joint implication is that attribution stability must be \emph{measured
against a null}, not presumed. This requirement motivates the dither condition
in Arm~A and the decode-path control in Arm~B, and it supplies the criterion
against which we evaluate our own claims --- including one we withdraw
(Section~\ref{sec:withdrawn}).

\subsection{Audio language models}
Evaluation of large audio language models (LALMs) has expanded
rapidly~\cite{chu2024qwen2audio,lee2025ahelm,luo2026survey}, with robustness
work concentrating on adversarial manipulation and on naturally occurring
corruptions such as noise and reverberation~\cite{li2025audiotrust}. Two gaps
persist. We identify no prior study that separates container from codec under a
verified signal-invariance constraint. More elementary: across the corpus we
surveyed, \emph{no study reports the container format in which audio was
submitted}. Corpus, prompt, and occasionally sampling rate are specified; the
delivery representation is not. Lee et al.~\cite{lee2025ahelm} document that
cross-model comparison is already confounded by unreported variation in
prompting and inference configuration; input representation belongs in the same
category of under-reported experimental degrees of freedom.

On what these systems attend to, the evidence is mixed. Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2025listen} report systematic \emph{lexical dominance} across six
LALMs, with models defaulting to \ttf{neutral} when lexical content is
affectively neutral and approaching chance in purely paralinguistic conditions.
Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026voxemo} provide a counterweight at a scale
inconsistent with chance-level competence, and identify zero-shot stochasticity
in generative readouts as a first-order threat to evaluation validity. Our Arm~A
encounters both phenomena directly (Section~\ref{sec:armA-task}).

\subsection{Sensitivity, criterion, and calibration}
Our principal Arm~A finding is most precisely expressed in signal-detection
terms. \emph{Sensitivity} is the separability of the class-conditional response
distributions, operationalised as the area under the ROC curve, and is invariant
to threshold placement. \emph{Criterion} is that placement, and can translate
without any change in separability. The two are independently manipulable:
Guo et al.~\cite{guo2017calibration} established that modern networks are
frequently miscalibrated and that calibration error varies independently of
accuracy, and related properties have been examined in language
models~\cite{kadavath2022know}. A manipulation leaving accuracy invariant may
still displace the operating point of any system that thresholds on posterior
probability.

\subsection{Where the gap lies}
Coding effects are characterised for discriminative speech models but not for
LALMs. Attribution fragility is characterised for vision under adversarial
perturbation but not for audio under ecologically routine degradation. No prior
work isolates the container under a verified invariance, and none calibrates
attribution divergence against a null perturbation. Critically for the present
paper, no prior work places an opaque and a transparent model class on the
identical manipulation, which is what licenses attributing a difference in
explanation robustness to model family rather than to a difference in the signal
chain.

\subsection{Rationale}
A single experiment cannot answer the question, because the two things we need
to separate --- what the codec does to the signal, and what the model does with
the codec --- are confounded within any one model class. An opaque model
supports only perturbation-based attribution, whose estimator variance is
unknown a priori; a transparent model supports exact attribution but constrains
the hypothesis space to hand-designed features. Running both on the identical
manipulation converts the difference between them from a nuisance into the
measurement.

Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} shows the design. Two properties are load-bearing.
First, \emph{single ancestry}: every condition in both arms is generated from
one canonical reference $x$, so the format contrast is the only systematic
difference between conditions. Second, \emph{per-arm null calibration}: each arm
carries a manipulation that is informationally empty by construction, against
which its effects are evaluated. Without the second property an observed
divergence between two attribution maps is uninterpretable, because estimator
variance and effect are not distinguishable.

\subsection{Corpus and reference construction}
Both arms draw on CREMA-D~\cite{cao2014cremad}: 7{,}442 clips from 91 actors,
each delivering one of twelve fixed sentences in one of six acted emotions
(anger, disgust, fear, happiness, neutral, sadness), with crowd ratings
collected separately for voice-only, face-only, and audio-visual presentation.

The fixed lexical inventory is methodologically load-bearing. Holding the words
constant across emotions and conditions removes the lexical channel as a source
of discriminative variance, so any recovered signal must be carried by acoustic
realisation. The same property makes the corpus maximally exposed to the
lexical-dominance collapse Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025listen} describe --- a
prediction Arm~A confirms.

The canonical reference is \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz} monophonic 16-bit linear PCM,
loudness-normalised to EBU~R128 ($I=\SI{-23}{\lufs}$, LRA${}=7$,
TP${}=\SI{-2}{\decibel}$). Coded conditions are produced from that reference by
a single encoder invocation each, and all decoding in Arm~B passes through one
identical FFmpeg call so that the decoder never confounds the codec.

\subsection{The identification strategy}
The container alters the byte layout; the codec alters the waveform. The design
separates them by exploiting an equality that can be verified rather than
assumed: the decoded PCM of a coded file and the PCM of that file re-wrapped as
WAV are the same signal. Table~\ref{tab:estimands} states the resulting
contrasts.

In Arm~A the invariance claim was verified directly: decoding both members of
each pair through an identical fixed path and comparing SHA-256 digests of the
resulting sample sequences yields \textbf{150/150 pairs bit-identical}. In Arm~B
the corresponding control (AAC output routed through an MP4 container versus
through a WAV container) is not bit-identical, because the WAV path passes
through an additional int16 quantisation, but its median standardised feature
difference is $|\mathrm{SMD}|=7.6\times10^{-5}$ with only 12 of 436 features
exceeding $0.05$. That figure is the noise floor of Arm~B: any effect larger
than it is a codec effect, not a decoding artefact.

The two panels tell a consistent story from opposite directions. Perceptual bit
allocation abandons the top octave, which Arm~A sees as a
\SI{12.4}{\decibel} excess of band-limited distortion and Arm~B sees as a single
descriptor of that same octave moving by roughly 38 standard deviations. The
distinction matters later: measured in \emph{training-set} standard deviations,
the shift is $38.6\sigma$, meaning the feature arrives at inference time in a
range no model has ever seen.

AAC's contribution is different in kind. Its \SI{32.68}{\milli\second} of
priming shifts frame alignment uniformly, which perturbs every first-order
temporal derivative a little rather than destroying one band. The largest AAC
shifts are consequently all delta-MFCC means (\ttf{mfcc\_d1\_01\_mean},
$\mathrm{SMD}=-4.16$). We flag a caveat here rather than in the limitations
section, because it bears on how the number should be read: delta means sit near
zero with small standard deviations, so their standardised shifts are inflated
relative to their absolute change. The duration measurement is the harder
evidence for the padding mechanism.

\section{Arm A: An Opaque Audio Language Model}\label{sec:armA}

\subsection{System and constraints}
The system under test is \ttf{gpt-audio-1.5}, accessed through an
OpenAI-compatible Azure AI Foundry endpoint at temperature $0$ with a fixed
seed. Audio is transmitted as base64 bytes with a MIME format declaration;
filenames and URIs are never transmitted, as either would leak condition
identity into the prompt.

Two endpoint constraints shaped the design and must be reported because they
bound the arm's scope. The deployment rejects text-only requests, requiring an
audio part on every call. It also rejects MP4/AAC payloads, accepting only WAV
and MP3 --- which fixes Arm~A's contrast as WAV versus MP3 and is the reason no
AAC condition appears in this arm. Notably, submitting MP4 bytes under a
\ttf{wav} format declaration elicits a different error from submitting an
unsupported format string, indicating that the service validates container
structure rather than trusting the declaration.

\subsection{Task selection and the readout}\label{sec:armA-task}
The initial formulation used the standard six-way categorical emotion task and
failed: the model emitted \ttf{neutral} on 87.7\,\% of trials and achieved
$0.20$ accuracy against a $0.167$ chance rate, with 14/350 responses
unparseable. This reproduces the lexical-dominance pattern Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2025listen} predict for affectively neutral lexical content, and
it is fatal for our purposes: a task on which the system does not perform above
chance cannot express a format effect, and attribution over a constant response
is undefined.

We evaluated three binary reformulations on uncompressed audio, scored on
bias-invariant discriminability rather than accuracy. Valence attained the
highest nominal accuracy ($0.81$), which is precisely the pathology --- it
reproduces the majority-class base rate $34/42$ exactly. Arousal partitions the
six categories evenly (high: angry, fearful, happy; low: sad, neutral,
disgusted), fixing chance at $0.50$, and corresponds to the affective dimension
most directly encoded in intensity, $F_0$, and speech rate~\cite{russell1980circumplex}
--- the correlates most exposed to coding. Arousal was selected.

The dependent variable is the renormalised posterior over the response
alternatives extracted from returned log-probabilities. Critically, the
statistic is $P(\text{high})$ --- the mass on a \emph{fixed} class label, not on
the correct one. The model emits \ttf{low} on 86.9\,\% of trials; under a
correct-label readout that bias alone inflates the statistic, whereas under a
fixed-label readout a constant responder attains $\mathrm{AUC}=0.5$ exactly, so
bias is provably uninformative about the estimand. Extraction restricts to
tokens with a non-null \ttf{bytes} field, because the returned distribution
places most of its mass on fifteen distinct non-emittable special tokens that
render identically as \ttf{<|end|>} and would otherwise deflate all posteriors.

Prompt selection required two rounds. The initial arousal prompt elicited
28\,\% refusals of the form \emph{``please provide the audio you'd like me to
listen to''}. Placing the audio part before the text eliminated refusals but
induced structured JSON emission with embedded chain-of-thought, destroying the
single-token decision position the readout requires --- and that reasoning
quoted \emph{transcribed lexical content}, which is independent evidence for
lexical dominance. An explicit non-solicitation clause eliminated refusals
without disturbing the response format. The final prompt yields 0\,\% refusals,
0 unparseable responses, and 50/50 usable utterances.

\subsection{Occlusion attribution}\label{sec:armA-attr}
For utterance $x$ and mask $m_j$, attribution for region $j$ is the induced
posterior decrement
\begin{equation}
a_j(x) = P(\text{high}\mid x) - P(\text{high}\mid m_j(x)),
\end{equation}
so positive $a_j$ indicates that suppressing region $j$ removes evidence
supporting the \ttf{high} response. The mask set comprises ten equal-duration
temporal windows (each replaced by noise matched to the utterance's long-term
average spectrum, scaled to local RMS, with \SI{10}{\milli\second} raised-cosine
crossfades), six band-stop filters on acoustically interpretable boundaries
(0--250, 250--500, 500--1000, 1000--2000, 2000--4000, \SIrange{4000}{8000}{\hertz}),
and one minimum-energy null mask that bounds the attribution attributable to the
masking operation itself.

Masks are applied to the canonical reference and each format condition is
regenerated from the masked signal, preserving single ancestry: the masked
stimulus in condition $c$ is the representation $c$ would take had that region
been absent, not a post-hoc corruption of an already-degraded signal.

In total the arm consumed 8{,}842 queries (8{,}016 unique calls, 0 errors);
responses are content-addressed by a digest over deployment, audio bytes,
prompt, and decoding parameters, rendering the arm re-executable from cache.

\subsubsection{Sensitivity is invariant}
Every bootstrap interval covers the uncompressed estimate of $0.734$. The
accuracy decrement from $0.68$ to $0.58$ at \SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second} does
not attain significance: McNemar's exact test~\cite{mcnemar1947} on discordant
pairs yields 6 utterances correct only under \ttf{ref} against 1 correct only
under \ttf{mp3\_64} ($p=.125$); at 32 and \SI{128}{\kilo\bit\per\second},
5 versus 1 ($p=.219$) and 4 versus 1 ($p=.375$). The sign is consistent across
the ladder, which suggests a genuine small effect, but the design is underpowered
at $n=50$ to resolve it. We report no detectable loss of sensitivity, not the
absence of one.

\subsubsection{The criterion is displaced}
Marginal $P(\text{high})$ declines from $0.208$ to $0.144$, a paired shift of
$-0.064$ ($p=6.8\times10^{-5}$, Wilcoxon signed-rank). Decisively, both
class-conditional distributions translate in the same direction and by
comparable amounts --- high-arousal utterances from $0.366$ to $0.252$,
low-arousal from $0.050$ to $0.036$ --- leaving their separation intact. In
signal-detection terms this is a criterion shift with sensitivity held constant.
It is a calibration phenomenon in the sense of Guo et
al.~\cite{guo2017calibration}, and it is by construction invisible to any
accuracy-based metric.

\subsubsection{The container contributes nothing}
Table~\ref{tab:armA-null} normalises every effect by the measured null floor.
Substituting an MP3 for its bit-identical decoded WAV displaces the posterior
\emph{less} than adding one least significant bit of noise. The correct
inference is not that the container has a small effect but that it has no
identified effect: the model exhibits mild generic sensitivity to waveform
perturbation, and the container difference lies entirely within that envelope.

\subsubsection{Attribution is substantially reordered}
Three inferences follow from the right-hand block of
Table~\ref{tab:armA-null}. The estimator floor is $\rho=0.699$, not unity: an
inaudible perturbation already dissipates roughly 30\,\% of the rank ordering,
so occlusion attribution on this system is substantially unstable and any
format-effect claim advanced without this calibration would conflate estimator
variance with effect. The codec effect nonetheless exceeds that floor decisively
($\rho=0.491$, displacement $0.208$, $p=4.2\times10^{-8}$), corroborated by an
independent set-overlap statistic --- fewer than half of the three most
influential regions are preserved. The container effect does not
($p=.478$), consistent with the posterior-domain result.

\subsubsection{Why sensitivity survives}
Fig.~\ref{fig:bands} supplies the mechanism. Model reliance concentrates
unambiguously in the \SIrange{500}{2000}{\hertz} octave --- the region
carrying the bulk of speech energy and the first two formants, and the canonical
locus of vocal effort. That a model-agnostic perturbation procedure recovers an
acoustically principled region rather than an arbitrary one is itself evidence
that the attribution operator is measuring something structured; fourteen of
sixteen regions yield positive attribution, the theoretically expected
direction.

The codec largely spares the discriminative evidence, which preserves

trend. The absence of dose-response is not a failed manipulation; it
independently reproduces the central claim of Wu et al.~\cite{wu2024codecsuperb}.

\section{Arm B: Interpretable Models Over Named Features}\label{sec:armB}

\subsection{Feature representation}
Each file is reduced to 436 named descriptors in four families, every
frame-level contour collapsed to eight order statistics (mean, median, standard
deviation, IQR, min, max, skew, kurtosis):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{spectral} (114): centroid, bandwidth, rolloff (85/95\,\%),
flatness, flux, entropy, slope, zero-crossing rate, RMS, seven-band spectral
contrast, eight band-energy ratios, and explicit high-frequency survival ratios
above 4 and \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz};
\item \textbf{cepstral} (240): 20 MFCCs with $\Delta$ and $\Delta\Delta$;
\item \textbf{prosodic} (56), computed in Praat via
Parselmouth~\cite{jadoul2018parselmouth}: $F_0$ statistics and slope, jitter
(local, RAP, PPQ5, DDP), shimmer (local, dB, APQ3, APQ5, APQ11, DDA), HNR,
formants $F_1$--$F_5$ with bandwidths, intensity, voiced-segment timing, and
smoothed cepstral peak prominence (CPPS);
\item \textbf{chroma} (24) and two global descriptors (duration, peak
amplitude).
\end{itemize}

Spectral and cepstral features are computed with
librosa~\cite{mcfee2015librosa} at 512-point FFT,
160-sample hop, 400-sample window. Chroma tuning is pinned to
zero rather than estimated, because an estimated tuning could itself shift under
compression and would silently become part of the effect being measured.

The design choice worth defending is that these features are \emph{named}. An
attribution over \ttf{prosody\_cpps} is a claim about breathiness; an
attribution over a learned embedding dimension is not a claim about anything.
This is what makes the enter/leave analysis of Section~\ref{sec:relocation}
possible, and it is the analytical capability Arm~A structurally lacks.

Extraction over $7{,}442\times4=29{,}768$ files failed on exactly one clip in
all four conditions, \ttf{1076\_MTI\_SAD\_XX}, which the corpus documentation
lists as having no audio. The remaining 7{,}441 clips form the analysis set.

\subsection{Protocol}
Actors are partitioned 60/11/20 into train, validation, and test with sex
balance preserved, yielding 4{,}905/896/1{,}640 clips. Speaker-independent
splitting is not a nicety here. A variance decomposition over the feature table
gives mean $\eta^2=0.178$ for speaker against $0.098$ for emotion, with 340 of
436 features speaker-dominant; a random row split would leak voice identity into
the test set and inflate every score reported below.

Fourteen scikit-learn classifiers spanning tree ensembles~\cite{breiman2001rf,chen2016xgboost},
kernel machines~\cite{cortes1995svm}, linear and discriminant models,
instance-based and probabilistic baselines~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}, plus a
PyTorch MLP --- fifteen trained models in all --- (512-256-128 with batch normalisation and dropout 0.3, AdamW with
cosine schedule, class-weighted cross-entropy with label smoothing, early
stopping on validation UAR), are trained on the uncompressed condition and
evaluated on all four. Item alignment is enforced: the same clips appear in
every condition, so a cross-format comparison differs only in format.

For reference throughout: CREMA-D's own crowd raters, hearing audio alone,
matched the actor's intended emotion on only 45.5\,\% of clips, with per-class
recall ranging from 0.966 (neutral) to 0.164 (sad). Models trained on the
\emph{intended} label can exceed this because they exploit systematic acting
cues human listeners do not consciously decode; the figure is reported as
context for absolute accuracy, not as a target.

The mechanism is legible because the features are named. A decision tree asks
whether \ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean} exceeds a threshold near 17; the answer
is unchanged whether the feature reads 16.4 or 46.4. A linear or kernel model
multiplies that $38\sigma$ excursion straight into its decision function. This
is textbook covariate shift~\cite{morenotorres2012shift}, but with an unusual
property: the shifted variable is not a proxy for anything the label depends on,
so the shift is pure contamination.

MP4/AAC behaves differently, costing everyone something and nobody very much
(0.7 to 11 points regardless of family), consistent with the diffuse padding
mechanism of Fig.~\ref{fig:signal}(c). Container routing has no material effect:
across the fifteen models the median difference between \ttf{roundtrip} and
\ttf{mp4\_aac64} is $0.003$ balanced accuracy and the maximum is $0.010$
($k$NN), against codec effects one to two orders of magnitude larger.
For a feature pipeline that decodes both itself, this contrast is a null by
construction --- and confirming the null is exactly what licenses attributing
everything else to the codec.

One further observation constrains the available remedies. Trained on
\ttf{mp4\_aac64} instead of \ttf{ref}, the RBF-SVM scores $0.572$ on AAC and
logistic regression $0.570$, against $0.586$ and $0.567$ clean-on-clean. The
emotional information survives \SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second} AAC essentially
intact; what fails is the train/serve mismatch. But training on AAC does
\emph{not} rescue MP3 --- the SVM falls to $0.167$, exactly chance --- so
matched-format training is a format-specific fix, not a general immunisation.

\subsection{What the models attribute to}
Attribution was computed on the held-out speakers using TreeSHAP for the
ensembles~\cite{lundberg2020treeshap}, LinearSHAP for the linear models, LIME
for local surrogates~\cite{ribeiro2016lime}, permutation importance, partial
dependence, a depth-4 global surrogate tree, and, for the network, six methods
from Captum~\cite{kokhlikyan2020captum}: Saliency, Input$\times$Gradient,
Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017ig},
DeepLIFT~\cite{shrikumar2017deeplift}, GradientSHAP, and Feature Ablation.

\subsubsection{Convergent evidence across families}
On uncompressed audio, five model families and four attribution algorithms
select the same acoustic evidence. The top SHAP features for XGBoost and
LightGBM are identical in the first five (\ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std},
\ttf{mfcc\_d2\_00\_std}, \ttf{duration\_s}, \ttf{prosody\_intensity\_std},
\ttf{mfcc\_00\_std}); the decision tree leads with
\ttf{prosody\_intensity\_std}; and all six gradient methods on the network
return \ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std}, \ttf{mfcc\_d2\_00\_std}, \ttf{prosody\_cpps},
and \ttf{duration\_s}. The two leaders are the standard deviations of the first
and second derivative of MFCC-0, which is to say \emph{how variable the rate of
change of overall loudness is} --- a direct formalisation of vocal agitation.
Per-emotion attributions are equally sensible: CPPS (a breathiness correlate)
drives both sadness and neutrality, $F_0$ median drives fear, and $F_2$ movement
drives disgust. The network was told none of this.

\subsubsection{The methods disagree with each other}\label{sec:methods-disagree}
Fig.~\ref{fig:methods}(a) is the uncomfortable result. SHAP agrees closely with
a model's \emph{intrinsic} importance for the simpler models ($\rho=0.98$ for
both the decision tree and logistic regression), but LIME and permutation
importance are close to unrelated to it. On the decision tree, LIME and SHAP
rank features at $\rho=0.008$ --- no relationship at all --- while still sharing
10 of their top 20. This reproduces the disagreement problem Krishna et
al.~\cite{krishna2022disagreement} document, on a model simple enough that no
approximation error can be blamed.

Panel~(b) sharpens the point rather than softening it. The six gradient-family
methods on the network agree almost perfectly ($\rho\geq0.94$ pairwise, with
Integrated Gradients and DeepLIFT at $0.998$). High agreement within a method
family is therefore not evidence of correctness --- these methods share a
mathematical lineage, and their consensus reflects that lineage. The practical
consequence is that a single-method XAI claim about which acoustic features
matter is not reproducible across methods. Only features surviving all four
independent views should be reported as robust; here that set is
\ttf{mfcc\_d1\_00\_std}, \ttf{prosody\_intensity\_std}, \ttf{duration\_s},
\ttf{prosody\_cpps}, and \ttf{prosody\_f0\_median}.

Two further calibrations belong with this result. A depth-4 global surrogate
reproduces its target model's predictions only 57 to 74\,\% of the time
(random forest 73.9\,\%, LightGBM 63.4\,\%, XGBoost 62.1\,\%, SVM 58.7\,\%,
logistic regression 57.1\,\%), so surrogate rules summarise a majority of a
model, not a model; reporting them without their fidelity would overstate what
has been explained. And the network's attributions pass the Adebayo
model-randomisation check (Fig.~\ref{fig:sanity}): correlation between
attributions from the trained network and a randomly initialised one of
identical architecture ranges from $-0.011$ to $0.206$ across all six methods.
These explanations describe the trained model, not merely the input
distribution.

What replaces what is the whole story, and it is readable only because the
features are named. Fig.~\ref{fig:synthesis}(b) lists the migration. Under MP3
the features \emph{entering} the network's top 25 are almost all encoder
artefacts --- \ttf{spectral\_flatness} in five statistics, plus
\ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean} --- while the features \emph{leaving} are
genuine voice-quality and prosodic measures: \ttf{prosody\_cpps},
\ttf{prosody\_f0\_skew}, \ttf{prosody\_f2\_mean}, \ttf{prosody\_f2\_std},
\ttf{prosody\_f3\_bw\_median}. The compressed model stops listening to the voice
and starts listening to the encoder. Under AAC the same displacement occurs in
milder form, with the padding-shifted \ttf{mfcc\_d1\_*\_mean} entering and $F_0$
statistics leaving.

\subsubsection{A blind spot in global attribution metrics}\label{sec:blindspot}
The logistic regression row of Table~\ref{tab:stability} is the most important
negative result in this paper. Its SHAP ranking is essentially unchanged under
MP3 ($\rho=0.987$, 22 of 25 top features retained) while its balanced accuracy
collapses from $0.567$ to $0.233$. The coefficients did not change; only the
inputs went out of range, and a global mean-$|$SHAP$|$ ranking is largely blind
to that.

This is a caution about the method, not about the model. \textbf{A global
attribution-stability metric can report ``nothing changed'' while the classifier
has effectively stopped working.} Any deployment that monitors explanation
stability as a proxy for model health must pair it with input-range monitoring
or local, per-instance attribution; the global ranking alone will not raise the
alarm.

\subsection{Diagnosis and repair}\label{sec:neutralise}
Because Arm~B's features are named, the failure is not merely observable but
addressable. Features were ranked by how far each codec moves them in units of
\emph{training} standard deviation, then progressively replaced with training
medians in both the compressed and the clean test set --- the clean column
controlling for whatever real signal the masking itself destroys.

the AAC case behaves as its different mechanism predicts: no single feature
dominates, with the network's best recovery arriving at 10 features masked
($0.513\rightarrow0.560$) and the SVM's at 40 ($0.499\rightarrow0.542$).

The diagnosis is therefore covariate shift on a small set of identifiable
descriptors, not loss of emotional information. That distinction is actionable,
and the ordering of remedies follows from it: use a threshold-based model
family, which is free; or clamp the top-band spectral descriptors, which
recovers almost everything at the cost of one feature; or retrain on the target
codec, which works but requires knowing the codec in advance and does not
transfer between codecs.

\subsection{Explanation displacement exceeds accuracy displacement}
Fig.~\ref{fig:synthesis}(a) places every model from both arms on one plane.
Reading it requires one caution, which we state before the finding: the two
arms' vertical coordinates are not the same measurement. Arm~A's $\rho$ is a
per-utterance rank correlation over 16 spectro-temporal regions estimated by
perturbation, with a measured null floor of $0.699$; Arm~B's is a global rank
correlation over 436 features computed exactly, with a floor of unity. The
figure places them on shared axes to compare \emph{magnitudes of displacement
relative to each estimator's own floor}, not to assert numerical
commensurability.

With that caution, the pattern is consistent. Arm~A occupies the top-left:
displacement of $0.509$ against a floor of $0.301$, with an accuracy change that
does not attain significance. This is the strong Ghorbani-style dissociation ---
the evidence moved and the decision did not.

Arm~B does not reproduce the strong form. Every condition that moves its
attributions also moves its accuracy, in the same direction and the same rank
order across models. What does hold, and holds throughout, is the weaker and
still consequential claim: \emph{the accuracy delta systematically understates
the attribution delta}. XGBoost loses 2.2 accuracy points on AAC and still
swaps 3 of its top 25 features; the network loses 8.7 points and swaps 9. An
accuracy-only benchmark would call the first negligible and the second modest.

Why the strong form appears in one arm and not the other is, we think, a
property of the representation rather than of the model's sophistication. Arm~B
attributes over hand-designed descriptors, so a codec artefact enters as a
specific out-of-range column that simultaneously corrupts the decision function
of any model that multiplies it --- coupling the two axes. Arm~A's model learns
its own representation end to end, and its attribution operator addresses
spectro-temporal regions rather than features, so evidence can be reorganised
across regions without the decision boundary being crossed. The dissociation is
therefore more likely, not less, in exactly the deployed end-to-end systems
where it is hardest to detect.

becomes less inspectable. Tree ensembles $\rho\geq0.988$; linear models
$0.987$; the neural network $0.677$; the audio language model $0.491$ against a
floor of $0.699$.

We resist the temptation to read this as a law. Three confounds are present:
attribution operator, feature representation, and model family all covary along
the ordering, and the two arms differ in task, sample size, and label space.
What the ordering does establish is that model family is a first-order
determinant of explanation robustness --- within Arm~B, where operator and
representation are held fixed, the network's $\rho=0.677$ against the trees'
$\geq0.988$ is a clean within-arm contrast on identical inputs and splits.

\subsection{One physical cause, two behavioural outcomes}
The arms agree precisely on the physics and diverge on the consequence, and this
is the result neither arm could have produced alone.

Both localise the damage to the same place. Arm~A measures the coding error
directly and finds \SIrange{4}{8}{\kilo\hertz} distorted \SI{12.4}{\decibel}
more than the \SIrange{500}{2000}{\hertz} octave. Arm~B measures the induced
feature shift and finds that the single most-displaced descriptor of 436 is the
one summarising \SIrange{6}{8}{\kilo\hertz}, at $38.6$ training standard
deviations. Two entirely different instruments, one location.

The behavioural consequence then splits on how each model consumes that band. In
Arm~A the band carries only moderate reliance (attribution $0.058$ against
$0.095$ for the mid-spectrum), so sensitivity survives and only the attribution
profile reorganises. In Arm~B the band enters as an explicit descriptor; models
that threshold it are unaffected, and models that multiply it lose up to 38
points. The codec did the same thing in both cases. What differed was the
consumer.

This also resolves an apparent tension with the literature. Reddy and
Vijayarajan~\cite{reddy2020compression} report modest, front-end-dependent codec
effects on SER; we report catastrophic effects for half our models. Both are
correct: the magnitude of a codec effect is not a property of the codec, and
reporting it without specifying the model family is reporting an average over a
bimodal distribution.

\subsection{The container is inert, twice}
The most robustly replicated result in this study is a null. Arm~A: substituting
an MP3 for its bit-identical decoded WAV displaces the posterior by
$0.83\times$ the inaudible floor and leaves attribution statistically
indistinguishable from the null ($p=.478$). Arm~B: re-wrapping AAC output as WAV
moves the fifteen models by a median of $0.003$ balanced accuracy, with a
maximum of $0.010$ --- against MP3 codec effects reaching $0.383$.

The two nulls have different epistemic status and both are worth having. Arm~B's
is close to structural --- once FFmpeg decodes both routes, the contrast is
nearly guaranteed --- and its value is as a pipeline validation that licenses
attributing everything else to the codec. Arm~A's is a genuine empirical result,
because an API-fed model has an opaque decode path that could in principle
differ by container, and the endpoint's container-structure validation shows it
inspects the wrapper. That it nonetheless behaves identically is informative.

The practical consequence is favourable: format conversion between containers,
absent re-encoding, appears behaviourally inert. The operational risk localises
entirely to the codec, which simplifies the mitigation problem considerably.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Attribution stability is a reportable robustness property}
The results support a specific and inexpensive recommendation. Systems that
surface per-instance rationales should report attribution stability across the
input representations they will actually encounter, alongside accuracy, and
should report it against a null. The two quantities are not redundant: in Arm~A
they dissociate completely, in Arm~B the accuracy figure understates the
attribution figure at every point, and in one case
(Section~\ref{sec:blindspot}) the attribution figure understates the accuracy
collapse. Neither dominates the other, which is precisely why both are needed.

\subsection{Null calibration is not optional}
The estimator floor we measure in Arm~A --- an inaudible perturbation
dissipating 30\,\% of attribution rank ordering --- implies that any claim of
the form ``manipulation $M$ alters the explanation'' is uninterpretable without
a null perturbation matched in every respect except informational content. We
propose this as standard practice for perturbation-based audio interpretability.
The cost is one additional condition; the benefit is that the claim becomes
falsifiable, as Section~\ref{sec:withdrawn} demonstrates at our own expense.

\subsection{Operational implications of criterion displacement}
Deployed systems frequently act on posterior probability rather than on the
argmax: escalation thresholds, triage routing, confidence-gated human review. A
uniform displacement of $0.064$ in posterior mass will alter the set of
instances crossing any fixed threshold, purely as a function of ingestion
format, while accuracy-based monitoring registers nothing. This yields a
directly testable operational prediction: organisations operating heterogeneous
ingestion paths should observe systematic differences in \emph{confidence
distributions} across those paths absent any difference in measured accuracy.

\subsection{Auditing and the archive problem}
Per-instance rationales are increasingly surfaced to operators and auditors. Our
results indicate that such rationales are not invariant across a transcoding
step that may leave the decision invariant. Where an audit is conducted on
archived compressed material while inference runs on uncompressed input --- or
the converse --- the decisions will agree while the explanations will not, with
under half the top-ranked regions in common in Arm~A and fewer than half the
top-25 features in common for the network in Arm~B. An explanation is not
reproducible unless the delivery format is part of the record.

\subsection{What named features buy}
Arm~B's mitigation (Section~\ref{sec:neutralise}) has no analogue in Arm~A, and
the asymmetry is structural rather than incidental. Identifying
\ttf{spectral\_contrast\_6\_mean} as the contaminated channel required
attribution over units with acoustic meaning; neutralising it required reaching
into the representation. Neither is possible through a chat-completions
endpoint, where the only available lever is the input waveform.

This is a narrower claim than Rudin's~\cite{rudin2019stop}. We are not arguing
that interpretable models should replace black boxes --- Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}
shows the single interpretable model in our zoo costing a third of achievable
performance, and the best model overall is a neural network. We are arguing that
a \emph{named-feature front end} retains a diagnostic and repair capability that
end-to-end pipelines lack, and that in codec-heterogeneous deployments this
capability has measurable value: 98\,\% of a 38-point loss, recovered by masking
one column.

\subsection{Corrections and threats to validity}\label{sec:withdrawn}
An earlier iteration of the Arm~A study reported a container effect: MP3
submission diverged from bit-identical decoded WAV on 47/47 utterances, at
approximately half the magnitude of the codec effect. This was advanced as the
principal finding. The supporting argument was that byte-identical requests
return bit-identical responses --- verified over five repetitions --- so the
measurement floor is exactly zero and any non-zero divergence constitutes
signal.

The argument is invalid in a way we believe generalises. \textbf{Zero variance
under byte-identical inputs does not bound the response to inputs that differ.}
The relevant floor is the divergence induced by an informationally null
modification. Once measured, that floor ($0.0459$) exceeds the container effect
within the same arm ($0.0379$). One qualification on scope: the earlier estimate
was computed on a different task and readout scale, so the magnitudes are not
directly commensurable and this is not a numerical refutation. What it
establishes is that the earlier study used an inappropriate control and
therefore never demonstrated that its effect exceeded any floor. We withdraw the
claim as unsupported rather than disproven. The general principle: a determinism
check is not a null control, and conflating them manufactures findings.

A second correction concerns Arm~B. The RBF-SVM has no exact explainer, so it
was given KernelSHAP with 100 sampled coalitions over 436 features. KernelSHAP
fits a weighted least-squares regression with one unknown per feature; with 100
coalitions for 436 unknowns that system is rank-deficient, and the result
diverged --- maximum mean $|$SHAP$|$ of $2.1\times10^{12}$ against a median of
$5.3\times10^{-4}$, with entire feature families assigned exactly zero. The
SVM's KernelSHAP ranking is excluded from every conclusion in this paper as an
estimation artefact, and the implementation now raises when the coalition budget
does not exceed the feature count. The SVM contributes accuracy and robustness
results but no attribution-stability row; its trustworthy views are permutation
importance and LIME, whose top features reproduce the consensus every other
model reaches.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Arm asymmetry.} The endpoint rejects MP4/AAC payloads, so Arm~A
covers MP3 only and Arm~B covers both codecs. All cross-arm claims are
restricted to the WAV-versus-MP3 contrast both arms share; MP4/AAC results are
Arm~B evidence and are labelled as such throughout. Closing this gap requires a
different deployment, not a different analysis.

\item \textbf{Attribution estimators are not commensurable across arms.} Arm~A
estimates by perturbation over 16 regions with a measured floor of $0.699$;
Arm~B computes exactly over 436 features with a floor of unity. The ordering in
Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} confounds operator, representation, and model family.
The clean contrast is within Arm~B, where all three are held fixed.

\item \textbf{Single system, single corpus, single language.} Arm~A uses 50
utterances from one commercial deployment; both arms use acted English affect
from one corpus. CREMA-D's human voice-only ceiling of 45.5\,\% shows the labels
are themselves only loosely recoverable from audio, and absolute accuracies do
not transfer to spontaneous speech.

\item \textbf{Arm A's sensitivity decrement is unresolved.} The accuracy decline
is directionally consistent across the bitrate ladder but non-significant at
every bitrate. Only the criterion shift and the attribution reordering are
supported.

\item \textbf{Effect magnitudes in Arm~A are modest.} The codec exceeds the null
floor by $1.47\times$ on the posterior measure, not by an order of magnitude.
The attribution effect is much stronger ($p=4.2\times10^{-8}$), but the two
should not be conflated.

\item \textbf{Arm B uses one split, not repeated cross-validation.} All results
come from a single deterministic 60/11/20 actor partition. Differences of a
point or two on the leaderboard should not be over-read; the codec effects are
10 to 38 points and are not at risk from split variance.

\item \textbf{Two bitrates in Arm~B.} Only \SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second} MP3 and
AAC were tested. The MP3 result is severe partly \emph{because}
\SI{64}{\kilo\bit\per\second} is aggressive for that codec; higher bitrates
would likely show a gradient. Arm~A's ladder covers 32/64/128 but is compressed
--- the 64-to-128 increment yields only \SI{0.46}{\decibel} of SNR, because the
psychoacoustic model saturates well below \SI{128}{\kilo\bit\per\second} at
\SI{16}{\kilo\hertz} monophonic input.

\item \textbf{Arm B discards time.} Every frame contour is collapsed to eight
order statistics, so no Arm~B attribution localises \emph{when} in the clip the
evidence sits. Arm~A's temporal masks address this, but only for the opaque
model.

\item \textbf{Arm B's attribution comparisons are global.} Spearman $\rho$ and
top-$k$ overlap over mean-$|$attribution$|$ are coarse instruments, and
Section~\ref{sec:blindspot} shows they can miss a total accuracy collapse. A
local, per-instance stability analysis would be a stronger instrument and is not
attempted here.

\item \textbf{Arm A's interpretable basis is contested.} Temporal windows and
spectral bands are the representation criticised
by~\cite{haunschmid2020audiolime,nasr2025beyond}; source separation, their
preferred alternative, is inapplicable to monophonic single-speaker material. We
mitigate --- bands follow acoustically interpretable boundaries, masks are
loudness-matched and edge-ramped, and a minimum-energy null mask bounds the
attribution attributable to masking itself --- but we do not dismiss the
objection.

\item \textbf{Occlusion indexes functional dependence, not mechanism.} It
licenses no claims about internal computation.

\item \textbf{Both arms are exploratory.} Task, prompt, readout, and null
control in Arm~A were selected after inspecting data from earlier arms.
Confirmatory status requires preregistered replication.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}
The highest-value extension is cross-system generalisation: replication across
multiple audio language models, commercial and open-weight. Open-weight systems
would permit gradient-based attribution and direct inspection of hidden
representations, adjudicating whether the reordering we observe reflects genuine
reorganisation of internal processing or is confined to the output surface ---
and would close the arm asymmetry by admitting AAC payloads.

Three further directions follow from specific results. The criterion-shift
mechanism yields a falsifiable prediction: any degradation attenuating
high-frequency transient content should displace the criterion toward
\ttf{low}, irrespective of whether it removes affective information, which is
testable with degradations matched on broadband SNR but differing in spectral
tilt. A null floor of $\rho=0.699$ is high, and since estimator noise can only
attenuate a true difference, averaging over randomised mask partitions would
likely \emph{increase} the measured codec effect. Finally, if coding operates as
an approximately additive criterion perturbation, a per-format affine
recalibration of the posterior should eliminate the shift; this is inexpensive
to test and immediately deployable.

Beyond arousal, speaker identity, vocal stress, and voice quality are carried by
precisely the spectral detail perceptual coding discards, and may exhibit the
sensitivity loss arousal does not.

\section{Conclusion}
We asked whether perceptual audio coding changes what a speech emotion model
relies on, as distinct from whether it changes what the model concludes. Across
two model classes at opposite ends of the transparency spectrum, sharing one
canonical reference signal and one coding manipulation, the answer is that it
changes the evidence more reliably than it changes the decision.

An audio language model retains its discriminative sensitivity under coding
while displacing its decision criterion by $0.064$ and reordering its attribution
profile to $\rho=0.491$ against a measured null floor of $0.699$. Fifteen
interpretable classifiers and networks show a graded version of the same
phenomenon: accuracy loss consistently under-reports attribution change, and
explanation stability is ordered by model family --- tree ensembles above
$0.988$, the network at $0.677$ --- on identical inputs and splits. The
container is inert in both arms. And the mechanism is the same in both: coding
abandons the top octave, which Arm~A sees as \SI{12.4}{\decibel} of excess
band-limited distortion and Arm~B sees as one named descriptor arriving $38.6$
training standard deviations out of range.

Two things follow that we would ask reviewers of affective computing systems to
adopt. Report the delivery format; it is an experimental degree of freedom
currently going unrecorded. And when reporting that an intervention changed a
model's explanation, report the divergence induced by an intervention carrying
no information, because without it the claim cannot be evaluated --- as we
demonstrate by withdrawing one of our own.
